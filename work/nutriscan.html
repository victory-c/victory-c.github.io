Case study — 2026

NutriScan

Food photo in, micronutrient estimate out — a four-stage deep-learning pipeline (segment → classify → portion → nutrients) trained and evaluated on a Slurm HPC cluster.

  • ▪Instance segmentation + 103-class food classifier
  • ▪Calibrated top-1 ~0.54 / top-5 ~0.74 on held-out test
  • ▪Champion checkpoints + reproducible eval discipline
  • PyTorch
  • Mask R-CNN + Swin
  • CLIP
  • Slurm

The problem

Calorie apps guess at macros from a text search. The harder, more useful question is micronutrients from a photo: what's actually on this plate, how much of it is there, and what does that mean in iron, potassium, or folate? NutriScan is my end-to-end attempt: a four-stage pipeline — segment → classify → portion → nutrient lookup — trained and evaluated on a university HPC cluster.

Demo

The pipeline

  • Stage 1 — segmentation. Mask R-CNN with a Swin transformer backbone finds every food instance in the photo. The champion checkpoint was selected on COCO segmentation mAP over a held-out split of FoodInsSeg (103 food classes), not on training loss.
  • Stage 2 — classification. A fine-tuned CLIP model labels each predicted crop. The champion reaches ~0.54 top-1 / ~0.74 top-5 on the test set — measured on predicted crops from stage 1, not ground-truth boxes, because that's what the model will see in production.
  • Stage 3 — portion estimation (in progress) and Stage 4 — nutrient lookup map labelled regions to grams and grams to micronutrients.

Eval discipline

The part of ML that doesn't fit in a tweet is the bookkeeping that makes results real:

  • Champion checkpoints. Every experiment competes against the reigning champion on the same held-out split; nothing is promoted on vibes. Three successor variants (different heads, harder augmentation) lost to the champion and stayed unpromoted.
  • Calibration before comparison. Raw softmax confidences lie. The deployed classifier applies temperature scaling fitted on validation data, so "0.9 confident" means something — and confidence thresholds in the app layer are honest.
  • Controlled experiments. Error analysis showed high-confidence cross-family confusions (french fries labelled carrot) that pointed at the crop representation, not the classifier: black-masked crops throw away plate context. The follow-up dataset keeps bbox context instead — built to within ±1 image of the original splits, so the comparison isolates exactly one variable.

Training on shared iron

Everything runs through Slurm on a shared cluster, which taught its own lessons. When a kernel upgrade left the GPU node unable to load its CUDA driver module for over a week, I stopped babysitting it: a self-rescheduling Slurm sentinel checks the device node every 30 minutes and submits the queued training job the moment the hardware comes back — with a flag file to prevent double-submission and a hard expiry so it can't zombie. Batch extraction fell back to CPU in the meantime. Long-running ML is an operations problem wearing a research costume.

What I learned

  • Evaluate on the pipeline's real inputs (predicted crops), not the convenient ones (ground-truth boxes) — the gap between the two is where production accuracy goes to die.
  • Calibrated probabilities are the difference between a confidence score you can build product logic on and a number that just feels good.
  • On shared infrastructure, automation that waits patiently beats a human who checks impatiently.
